# Circular flow number at most 11/2 for cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graphs of oddness at most 6

*Working notes: draft of a paper, 2026-09-19. Sources: `oddness.md` §§1–2, 6, 12, 18–24; `c6-model-spec.md`; `c6-independent-check.md`. Statements marked (computer) rest on the computations described in §6.*

## Abstract

Jaeger proved that cubic graphs of oddness at most 2 have nowhere-zero 5-flows, and Mazzuoccolo and Steffen proved the same for cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graphs of oddness 4. Their method builds, from a 2-factor with $2t$ odd circuits and a choice of one edge on each odd circuit, a family of $2^t$ vertex 2-colourings and shows that one of them is a balanced valuation. We show two things about the next case $t=3$. First, the method fails there as it stands: there are cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graphs (the smallest on 42 vertices) with a 2-factor of six odd circuits and a choice of edges for which none of the eight colourings is balanced at the value $\pm\frac53$ required for a 5-flow. Secondly, the *same* eight colourings always contain a balanced valuation at $\pm\frac{11}{7}$: every cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graph of oddness at most 6 has circular flow number at most $\frac{11}2$, and the instances above show that $\frac{11}{2}$ is exactly what the method yields. The second result is computer-assisted: it is reduced to the infeasibility of 5,292 small integer programs, which was established by two independent implementations with different solvers.

## 1. Introduction

Tutte's 5-flow conjecture says that every bridgeless graph has a nowhere-zero 5-flow; equivalently, that its circular flow number $\Phi_c$ is at most 5. Seymour's theorem gives $\Phi_c\le6$, and no general bound below 6 is known. It suffices to consider cubic graphs, and by Kochol a minimum counterexample is cyclically 6-edge-connected. The *oddness* $\omega(G)$ of a bridgeless cubic graph is the least number of odd circuits in a 2-factor. Known: $\Phi_c\le5$ if $\omega\le2$ (Jaeger), if $\omega=4$ and $G$ is cyclically 6-edge-connected (Mazzuoccolo–Steffen, "MS"), and if the cyclic connectivity is at least $\frac52\omega-3$ (Steffen); so a minimum counterexample to the conjecture has oddness at least 6.

> **Theorem 1.** Let $G$ be a cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graph with $\omega(G)\le6$. Then $\Phi_c(G)\le\frac{11}2$.

> **Theorem 2.** There is a cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graph on 42 vertices with a 2-factor consisting of six 7-circuits and a choice of 0-edges for which none of the eight flow-partition colourings is a balanced $\pm\frac53$ valuation, while the best of them is balanced exactly at $\pm\frac{11}7$. Further instances exist on 50, 54, 60 and 78 vertices. (All these graphs do have nowhere-zero 5-flows.)

Theorem 1 is, to our knowledge, the first bound below 6 for a class of graphs defined by oddness 6. Theorem 2 shows that the freedom the MS method does not use at oddness 4 (the position of the 0-edges, or the 2-factor) is indispensable at oddness 6, and that within the fixed template $\frac{11}2$ cannot be improved: the template has no values strictly between 5 and $\frac{11}2$ (Remark 3.3).

## 2. Balanced valuations and the flow-partition template

**Balanced valuations (Jaeger).** For a real number $r>2$, a cubic graph $G$ has a circular nowhere-zero $r$-flow if there is a partition $V=B\cup W$ (black, white) such that
$$ r\,|b_X-a_X|\le(r-2)\,|\partial X|\qquad\text{for all }X\subseteq V,\tag{1}$$
where $b_X=|X\cap B|$, $a_X=|X\cap W|$. (The valuation is $w=\pm\frac r{r-2}$; for $r=5$ it is $\pm\frac53$, for $r=\frac{11}2$ it is $\pm\frac{11}7$.) Condition (1) with $X=V$ forces $|B|=|W|$; hence $X$ and $V\setminus X$ violate (1) together, and we may always take a violating set to be *black-heavy*, $k:=b_X-a_X>0$. For a fixed partition, (1) is decided by one minimum-cut computation.

**The template (Steffen; MS).** Let $F_2$ be a 2-factor of $G$ with odd circuits $C_1,\dots,C_{2t}$, $M=E(G)\setminus E(F_2)$. Choose an edge on each odd circuit (the *0-edge*). Colour $M$ with 1, the even circuits alternately 2, 3, the 0-edges 0 and the rest of each odd circuit alternately $2,3,\dots,2,3$, starting next to the 0-edge. Exactly one vertex $z_i$ of $C_i$ has no colour-2 edge. $H$, the subgraph of colours 1 and 2, consists of $t$ paths joining the $z_i$ in pairs, each of odd length and starting and ending with colour 1, and of even circuits ("$H$-circuits"); colours 1 and 2 alternate along every component. The **template** is the set of proper 2-colourings of $H$: $2^{t+h}$ partitions, $h$ the number of $H$-circuits. MS show that these are balanced $\pm2$ valuations of a suitable 4-flow construction and that for $t\le2$ one of them satisfies (1) with $r=5$. In every template partition each component of $H$ is evenly split, so $|B|=|W|$.

## 3. Bad sets at a general value of $r$

Let $t=3$, fix $F_2$, the 0-edges and a template colouring $\varepsilon$. For $X\subseteq V$ write $m=|\partial X|$, $c_i$ for the number of colour-$i$ edges in $\partial X$, and $q$ for the number of black minus the number of white $z$'s in $X$.

**Lemma 3.1.** If $X$ is black-heavy with $k=b_X-a_X$, then $k\le c_1$, $k\le c_2+q$, $q\le3$, and $k\equiv m\equiv c_1\pmod2$. In particular $2k\le m+3$.

*Proof.* Matching edges inside $X$ join a black to a white vertex, so $k$ is at most the number of vertices of $X$ matched outside, which is $c_1$; and $c_1\equiv|X|\equiv m$ because $G$ is cubic. Likewise colour-2 edges inside $X$ are bichromatic; the vertices of $X$ not covered by them are the $X$-ends of the $c_2$ boundary edges of colour 2 and the $z$'s in $X$, so $k\le c_2+q$. The two ends of a path of $H$ have different colours, so $q\le3$. $\square$

**Lemma 3.2 (catalogue).** Let $G$ be cyclically 6-edge-connected with $n\ge10$. A black-heavy set $X$ violates (1) for $r=\frac{11}2$, i.e. $11k>7m$, if and only if $(m,k,c_1,c_2,q)$ is one of
$$(6,4,4,2,2),\qquad(6,4,4,1,3),\qquad(7,5,5,2,3).$$
In all three cases $k=c_1=c_2+q$. In the first case exactly two paths of $H$ have exactly one end in $X$, these two ends are black, and the third path has both or none of its ends in $X$; in the other two cases every path has exactly one end in $X$, and it is black. In the second case exactly one edge of $\partial X$ is not in $H$; in the other cases $\partial X\subseteq E(H)$.

*Proof.* From $2k\le m+3$ and $11k>7m$ we get $m\le10$. For $m\in\{8,9,10\}$ the conditions $k>\frac7{11}m$, $k\le\frac{m+3}2$, $k\equiv m$ have no solution; neither have they for $m\in\{4,5\}$; $m\le2$ does not occur since $G$ is 3-edge-connected; for $m=3$ they give $k=3$, but a 3-edge cut of $G$ is trivial ($|X|=1$, $k=1$). For $m=7$: $k=5$, $c_1\in\{5,7\}$, and $c_1=7$ would force $c_2=0$, $q\ge5$; so $c_1=5$, $c_2\le2$, $q\ge5-c_2$, giving $c_2=2$, $q=3$. For $m=6$: $k=4$, $c_1=6$ is impossible as before, so $c_1=4$, $c_2\le2$, $c_2+q\ge4$: $(c_2,q)\in\{(2,2),(2,3),(1,3)\}$. If $(c_2,q)=(2,3)$ then $\partial X\subseteq E(H)$ and all three paths cross $\partial X$ an odd number of times, every $H$-circuit an even number, total odd, contradicting $m=6$. The descriptions of the ends follow from $q$ and the fact that the ends of a path have opposite colours. $\square$

**Remark 3.3 (the template jumps from 5 to 11/2).** Running the same analysis at $r=5$ ($5k>3m$) adds exactly one type, $(11,7,7,4,3)$, whose ratio $k/m=\frac7{11}$ is the threshold $\frac{r-2}{r}$ of $r=\frac{11}2$. For $5\le r<\frac{11}2$ the set of bad types does not change; so the template, for a given instance, yields either $r=5$ or nothing better than $r=\frac{11}{2}$, unless a bad 6- or 7-cut forces more.

**Lemma 3.4 (tightness and alternation).** Let $X$ be bad for $\varepsilon$ as in Lemma 3.2. (T1) The $X$-end of every colour-1 and every colour-2 edge of $\partial X$ is black, every $z$ of a separated path in $X$ is black. (T2) Along a path $P$ of $H$, traversed from an end $s$, crossing edges alternate in colour; an edge leaving $X$ has colour 1 if $s$ is black and 2 if $s$ is white, an edge entering $X$ has colour 2 if $s$ is black and 1 if $s$ is white. A path with one end in $X$ crosses an odd number of times, the others an even number. (T3) Every $H$-circuit crosses $\partial X$ in equally many edges of colour 1 and of colour 2.

*Proof.* (T1) is the equality case of Lemma 3.1. The vertices of $P$ alternate in colour and its edges alternate $1,2,1,\dots$ starting with 1; the $t$-th edge $v_{t-1}v_t$ has colour 1 iff $t$ is odd; if it leaves $X$ then $v_{t-1}$ is black, i.e. $t-1$ is even iff $s$ is black. The other cases and (T3) are the same computation. $\square$

## 4. Reduction of Theorem 1 to finitely many configurations

If $\omega(G)\le4$, Theorem 1 follows from Jaeger and MS. Let $\omega(G)=6$ and take a 2-factor with six odd circuits; $n\ge10$ and cyclic 6-edge-connectivity give girth at least 6, so the odd circuits have length at least 7 and $n\ge42$. Fix the 0-edges arbitrarily, and fix the colouring of the $H$-circuits. We claim that one of the four colourings of the paths in which $z_1$ is black satisfies (1) with $r=\frac{11}2$. If not, each of these four colourings $\pi$ has a bad set $X_\pi$, by Lemma 3.2 of one of the following **14 kinds**: a 7-cut or a $(6,4,1,3)$-cut containing the three black ends; or, for a path $P_i$ and $p\in\{0,1\}$, a $(6,4,2,2)$-cut containing the black ends of the other two paths and both ($p=1$) or none ($p=0$) of the ends of $P_i$, where $P_i$ either does not cross $\partial X$ or crosses it (then at least twice). The data of Lemma 3.4 attached to each $X_\pi$ (memberships of the six $z$'s, the colour counts, for a crossing non-separated path the colour of its first vertex) make up an *item*, and the four items a *killer assignment*; there are $14^4=38{,}416$ of them. Note that all four items refer to the same colouring of the $H$-circuits.

A **configuration** is a list of items. Permuting the three paths, reversing a path, and exchanging black and white while replacing every set by its complement map a graph with a configuration to a graph with the relabelled configuration.

**Lemma 4.1 (computer; `c6_cover_completeness.py`).** There is a list $\mathcal L$ of 5,292 configurations, of 2, 3 or 4 items each, such that every one of the 38,416 killer assignments contains, after a suitable relabelling, a member of $\mathcal L$ as a sub-list.

($\mathcal L$ is described in `c6-model-spec.md` §4; it was originally obtained as the list of "minimal covers of four classes", a notion that is only correct in the absence of $H$-circuits; Lemma 4.1 is what is actually needed and was checked directly.)

**Proposition 4.2 (computer).** No member of $\mathcal L$ occurs in a cyclically 6-edge-connected cubic graph with the canonical colouring of a 2-factor with six odd circuits.

Theorem 1 follows: a failing instance would give a killer assignment, hence by Lemma 4.1 a relabelled instance containing a member of $\mathcal L$.

## 5. The relaxation behind Proposition 4.2

Given a configuration $S_1,\dots,S_J$ ($J\le4$), the vertex set splits into $2^J$ *atoms* according to membership in the $S_j$. We record only counts: the number of vertices of each atom; for each path of $H$, how many of its edges go from atom $a$ to atom $b$ (the colour of such an edge is forced by (T2) applied to every $S_j$ it crosses, and the variable is absent when two cuts force different colours); the numbers of $H$-circuit edges of each colour from a black end in $a$ to a white end in $b$ (by (T1) possible only if $a$ lies inside and $b$ outside every cut crossed); the position of the non-$H$ edge of each $(6,4,1,3)$-cut; and how the colour-2 and non-$H$ edges between atoms are distributed over the six odd circuits and the even circuits of $F_2$. The constraints, all of them necessary conditions, are: the paths are walks between the prescribed atoms with the right parity of crossings of each cut; each cut has its numbers $c_1,c_2$ of boundary edges, with equally many $H$-circuit edges of the two colours; in each atom the ends of $H$-circuit segments satisfy $B_1-B_2=W_1-W_2$; parity and size conditions for each atom coming from the perfect matching, from the colour-2 edges, and from the fact that circuits of $F_2$ are closed; each odd circuit is a closed walk through the atoms, it leaves the atom of its $z$ if that atom has at most 6 vertices, and if that atom has exactly 3 vertices (the $z$ and its two neighbours on the circuit) no other circuit meets it; and, for every union $U$ of atoms, the facts that $|\partial U|\le3\min(|U|,|\bar U|)$, that a set of at most 5 vertices is acyclic, so $|\partial U|\ge|U|+2$, and that $|\partial U|\ge6$ when both sides have at least 4 vertices. The full statement is `c6-model-spec.md` §§2–3.

This relaxation generalizes the uncrossing argument of MS: for two $(6,4,2,2)$-cuts on the same pair of paths with opposite signs it reproduces their contradiction. It cannot exclude configurations of 11-cuts (and must not, by Theorem 2), which is why it proves Theorem 1 and not the 5-flow statement.

## 6. The computations

1. **First implementation** (`tools/count_c6.py`, `tools/enum_c6.py`): OR-tools CP-SAT; union facts for single atoms (and pairs) up front, the others and the connectivity of walks separated lazily. Results: of the 834 pairs of items, 300 are infeasible (two lines: 36 of 66; line and crossed pair cut: 144 of 288; two crossed pair cuts: 120 of 216; every pair involving a 7-cut or a $(6,4,1,3)$-cut is feasible). Of the 5,292 members of $\mathcal L$, 4,380 contain an infeasible pair, 896 an infeasible triple, 16 are infeasible only as a whole. None is feasible or undecided.
2. **Second implementation** (`tools/c6_independent.py`), written from the specification alone without access to the first: mixed integer program solved by HiGHS, every feasible answer re-verified by an exact checker against all constraints and all $2^{2^J}$ unions; the 834 pairs additionally by z3 in exact integer arithmetic. It agrees with the first implementation on every configuration solved by both (all pairs, the 384 triples and four-item lists needed for the certificate, 150 random feasible triples) and certifies all 5,292 members of $\mathcal L$ by itself.
3. **Validation of the model on real data** (`tools/validate_c6.py`): bad sets of all four types were read off actual instances (the archived template failures and 16,500 random 0-edge choices on six graphs) and translated into configurations; all 549 configurations obtained (406 single items, 143 pairs, including 11-cuts) are feasible in the model, as they must be.
4. **Ablation.** Each of the 300 infeasible pairs becomes feasible if either the union facts or the circuit facts are dropped: the exclusions are genuinely of MS type (cyclic connectivity) combined with the closed-walk structure of the odd circuits.

## 7. The template fails at $r=5$ (Theorem 2)

`data/oddness6_template_counterexample_n42.json` (written out in `data/README.md`): a cubic graph on 42 vertices, girth 6, cyclically 6-edge-connected (verified by SAT), with a 2-factor of six 7-circuits and 0-edges such that $H$ is three paths and no $H$-circuit, and each of the eight colourings has a bad set of type $(11,7,7,4,3)$: a whole 7-circuit together with 3- or 5-vertex arcs around one end of each of the other two paths (13, 13, 13 and 15 vertices). 42 is the least possible order for six odd circuits in girth 6. Further instances: 50 vertices (circuits $7^6,8$), 54 vertices (two of the four killers are $(6,4,2,2)$-cuts crossed by their third path), 60 vertices (two instances), 78 vertices. Verification script: `tools/verify_fullkill.py`. On all 26 archived instances the best colouring has $\max_Xk/m=\frac7{11}$ exactly (`tools/template_ratio.py`), i.e. the template gives $\Phi_c\le\frac{11}2$ and nothing better at that choice of 0-edges.

These failures are isolated: on the 60-vertex instance exactly one of the $7^6$ choices of 0-edges fails, and in every known failure moving a single 0-edge repairs the template; equivalently (`exp-B-defect.md`) a nowhere-zero 5-flow is always found among the colourings of $H$ with exactly one monochromatic colour-2 edge. Whether some choice of 0-edges always works is open:

> **Problem.** Let $G$ be cyclically 6-edge-connected with a 2-factor of six odd circuits. Is there always a choice of 0-edges and a template colouring that is a balanced $\pm\frac53$ valuation?

## 8. Remarks and open ends

- A proof of Proposition 4.2 by hand is desirable at least for the 300 pairs, which carry 83 % of $\mathcal L$; see `oddness.md` §23 for the first steps in the simplest case.
- For $2t=8$ odd circuits the catalogue at the threshold $\frac23$ of $r=6$ consists of $(7,5)$ and $(8,6)$ only, and between $\frac7{11}$ and $\frac23$ it contains $(6,4)$, $(7,5)$, $(8,6)$ and $(12,8)$; by analogy with Theorem 2 one expects the 12-cuts to defeat the fixed template, so the method is not expected to give a bound below 6 at oddness 8 without using the 0-edges.
