# Experiment C — rounding inside the zonotope (2026-09-19)

Implements §18.3 of `oddness.md`. Script `tools/zono_round.py` (written by a subagent, which stalled twice before reporting; this reduced run was made by hand: single process, 20 restarts, graphs Petersen, $J_5$, $J_7$, $GP(13,3)$, $R_2$, the 42-vertex template counterexample `cx42`; $r\in\{8,6,\tfrac{11}2,5\}$ and $\tfrac92$ on the snarks). Tables below are generated by `tools/zono_round_report.py tools/zono_round_results.json`.

**Procedures.** `walk`$\delta$: Lovett–Meka-style random walk from $w=0$ inside $Z(G)\cap[-c,c]^V$, $c=r/(r-2)$, Gaussian steps of size $\delta c$ orthogonal to the frozen coordinates and to the indicator vectors of the cuts that became tight (a step leaving $Z(G)$ is shortened to the boundary, the blocking cut found by max-flow, constraints are sticky); `walkray`: every step runs to the next constraint; `lp`: iterative LP rounding in the flow formulation (random objective, freeze the coordinates at $\pm c$, repeat); `walk+lp`: walk, then LP rounding from the stuck point's frozen pattern. (A first version of the walk was unsound — its results are discarded, kept only in the scratch directory of the session.) Ground truth by CP-SAT: existence of a $\pm c$ vector, whether the frozen pattern of a stuck run extends to one ("extends"), and the minimum number of frozen signs to flip.

**Reading.**
1. *The rigidity predicted in §18.3 is exact:* in every stuck run (hundreds) every link of the maximal chain of tight sets contains at most one unfrozen vertex and all unfrozen coordinates lie in $\tfrac1b\mathbb Z$ ($b=3$ for $r=5$, $7$ for $r=\tfrac{11}2$, $2$ for $r=6$, $3$ for $r=8$).
2. *But the stuck configurations are not local.* The number of unfrozen coordinates is about $n/2$ on the larger graphs (median 22 of 42 on `cx42`, 19.5 of 40 on $R_2$ at $r=5$), the chain has 20–25 links with boundary sizes ranging over everything from 5 to 23, and the unfrozen vertices span the whole graph (their diameter equals the graph's). Only on Petersen is the stuck set small (2–4 vertices, all chain cuts of size $\le7$).
3. *Success rates.* The random walks essentially never reach $r\le6$ except on Petersen (and reach $r=8$ in a few per cent of runs). LP rounding is much better: it finds the exact optimum of the grid on Petersen ($5$), $J_5$ and $J_7$ ($\tfrac92$); on `cx42` it reaches $\tfrac{11}2$ (2/20) and not 5; on $R_2$ it reaches 6, and 5 once with walk+lp. At $r=8$ it succeeds in 30–100 % of runs.
4. *Frozen patterns are often wrong.* The frozen signs of a stuck run extend to a genuine $\pm c$ vector in roughly one to two thirds of the sampled cases; otherwise 1–3 (once 4) frozen signs have to be flipped.

So rounding that does not factor the group does work at $r=8$ with good probability and degrades steadily towards 5; there is no sign of a wall at 6, but also no sign of a finite local repair problem: a stuck point is a global object (half the vertices unfrozen, a chain through cuts of all sizes). As implemented this is not a proof strategy. What could still be tried: a potential-function (entropy / partial-colouring) analysis of the LP rounding at $r=8$, where it succeeds often, to see whether it yields *any* bound by this route.
